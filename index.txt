Partially trainable neural networks bridge fixed random features and fully adaptive representations. This paper quantifies a phase transition pattern in approximation bounds and observes analogous behavior in training errors: a rapid but smooth initial improvement followed by diminishing returns, with a sharper transition under greater initialization mismatch. Under polynomial spectral decay with exponent $α>1$ and leverage compatibility, we establish a squared $L_2$ approximation rate $\mathcal{O}((κ_P\log(m/δ)/m)^α)$ for two-layer networks of width $m$ and trainable proportion $P$, where $κ_P=(P/κ_1^*+(1-P)/κ_0^*)^{-1}$. The bound holds uniformly over the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) with probability at least $1-δ$. The constants $κ_0^*$ and $κ_1^*$ quantify the fixed and trainable groups' sampling mismatches relative to the target RKHS. For $d=5$ and $τ=10$, a trainable proportion of approximately $4.1\%$ captures $90\%$ of the reduction in the approximation bound from $P=0$ to $P=1$.
